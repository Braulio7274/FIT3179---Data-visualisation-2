UNDERSTANDING THE DATA

Two sources reveal different parts of Australia's story

Australia's cultural diversity can be explored from two complementary perspectives. The Australian Bureau of Statistics Census provides a detailed snapshot of where people live, where they were born and the languages spoken at home.

Settlement data from the Department of Home Affairs provides another perspective, showing how migrants arriving through Skilled, Family and Humanitarian streams are distributed throughout Australia.

01 ORIGINS & LANGUAGE

Australia's diversity begins with where people come from and the languages they speak

Australia's overseas-born population represents communities from across the world, while the languages spoken at home reveal another layer of cultural diversity.

Origins. The largest overseas-born communities come from several world regions, with Asian and European countries particularly prominent among the largest birthplace groups.

Language. Mandarin is the most widely spoken non-English language at home, while a broad range of other languages demonstrates the diversity within Australia's population.

02 GEOGRAPHY

Cultural diversity is concentrated differently across Australia

The overseas-born population is not distributed evenly. Some areas have a particularly high share of overseas-born residents, while Australia's largest population centres also contain the greatest absolute numbers.

Population share. The choropleth highlights areas where overseas-born residents make up a particularly large proportion of the local population.

Population size. The proportional-symbol map focuses on absolute numbers, revealing where the largest overseas-born populations live.

03 STATE DIFFERENCES

Cultural diversity takes a different form in every state and territory

Looking beyond national totals reveals important differences between states in both migration and language. Some states have higher overseas-born populations, while others stand out through particular language communities.

Language concentration. The same language can represent very different shares of the population depending on the state or territory.

Two measures of diversity. Being born overseas and speaking a language other than English at home are related measures, but they do not produce identical patterns across Australia.

A CLOSER COMPARISON

New South Wales and Victoria show distinct language profiles

Australia's two most populous states contain many of the same major language communities, but the size of those communities differs considerably.

Comparing the states. The butterfly chart makes it easier to identify languages that are relatively more prominent in New South Wales or Victoria.

04 GLOBAL CONNECTIONS

Australia's migration story connects local communities to the world

Recent settlement patterns demonstrate how migration links major origin countries with different Australian states.

Migration flows. Major source countries connect to several Australian destinations, with the largest flows generally directed towards the country's major population centres.

05 SETTLEMENT STREAMS

Different migration pathways contribute to Australia's states in different ways

Skilled, Family and Humanitarian migration streams vary greatly in size, and their settlers are distributed differently across Australian states and territories.

Settlement streams. Skilled migration represents the largest of the three streams, while New South Wales and Victoria receive the greatest overall numbers of permanent settlers.
