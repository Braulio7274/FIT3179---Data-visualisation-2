Cultural Diversity in Australia

How migration, language, birthplace and settlement patterns shape Australia’s cultural landscape.

Understanding the Data

This visualisation draws primarily on two data sources: Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Census data, and Department of Home Affairs settlement data.

ABS data is used to explore birthplace, languages spoken, and geographic patterns of diversity across Australia. Home Affairs settlement data is used to show how recent migration streams distribute across states and territories.

2 primary data sources
8 states and territories
2016–2025 settlement data period

Note: ABS Census data and settlement data measure different things. Census data describes Australia’s resident population, while settlement data tracks migrant arrivals and streams.

Australia’s Diversity Begins with Birthplace and Language

The composition of Australia’s population reflects decades of migration. Birthplace and language data show the scale of major communities and the range of cultures represented across the country.

The treemap highlights the largest overseas-born communities, showing how Australia’s diversity is shaped by both long-established and more recent migration patterns.

The ranked language chart complements this by showing the most widely spoken languages other than English, revealing the cultural and linguistic breadth of the population.

Diversity Is Not Evenly Distributed Across Australia

Cultural diversity varies substantially by location. Some areas have a very high concentration of overseas-born residents, while others remain much less internationally diverse.

The choropleth map emphasises the proportion of overseas-born residents in each SA2, making it easier to identify areas where diversity forms a large share of the local population.

The proportional symbol map instead highlights magnitude, helping show which places have the largest overseas-born populations in absolute terms.

Language Patterns Reveal Differences Between States

The composition of language communities differs across Australian states. Looking at these differences helps reveal how migration histories and settlement patterns vary regionally.

The heatmap provides a broad comparison of major language groups across states, allowing dominant and less prominent communities to be compared at a glance.

The butterfly chart then focuses more directly on differences between New South Wales and Victoria, showing how even the largest states have distinct linguistic profiles.

States Also Differ in Their Overall Diversity Profiles

Diversity is not only visible in individual communities. It can also be compared at the state level to show broader differences in the scale and structure of cultural variation.

The dumbbell chart supports direct comparison between states, making it easier to see which places stand out on selected diversity measures and where the largest gaps appear.

Migration Connects Australia to the World

Beyond the resident population, migration data helps reveal how people continue to move into Australia through different channels and toward different destinations.

The flow map shows how migrant movement links origin locations and Australian destinations, emphasising Australia’s continued role as a connected migration destination.

Different Migration Streams Reshape the Settlement Pattern

Not all migrants arrive through the same pathway. Skilled, family and humanitarian streams distribute differently across Australia, shaping the demographic profile of each state and territory.

The Sankey diagram shows how migration streams are distributed across Australia, making it easier to compare the relative importance of skilled, family and humanitarian settlement in each state.
